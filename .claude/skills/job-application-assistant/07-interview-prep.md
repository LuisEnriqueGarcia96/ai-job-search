# Interview Preparation Guide

## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do differently.

### 1. B2B e-commerce adoption: digital billing from 60% to 80% (product ownership, customer discovery)
**S:** At Alicorp, wholesale clients and distributors usually ordered through a company sales rep who visited them and entered the order in SAP. Clients had no autonomy: orders were slower to dispatch, they could not order on weekends, holidays or after hours, and they depended on the rep for account information such as credit line used, credit notes, discounted products and benefits. The B2B e-commerce platform carried about 60% of billing in the channel.
**T:** As Product Manager I owned the platform's quarterly backlog (Q4 2025 / 2026) and had to grow digital adoption.
**A:** I ran sessions with the wholesale channel stakeholders; the sales strategy and sales teams brought client needs, and with our UX/UI team we built prototypes and interviewed clients. We prioritized the purchase journey from cart to checkout: an asynchronous checkout so clients could keep browsing while the order was being created in SAP, visibility of applied discounts at checkout, and alternative product suggestions when an item was out of stock. I wrote the user stories in Jira, ran refinement and sprint planning with the Scrum team, and led UAT before each go-live.
**R:** Digital billing rose by 20 percentage points in Q4 2025, reaching 80% of total client billing in the channel.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a product you owned", "How do you discover user needs?", "How do you prioritize a backlog?", "Describe a measurable impact"

### 2. Truck routing SaaS rollout (change management, turning business constraints into system rules)
**S:** Transport managers at Alicorp's distributors assigned delivery points to trucks manually, using an Excel file and Google Maps to see which zones each truck covered.
**T:** As Digital Project Manager I had to stabilize a routing SaaS with an external partner and get the transport teams to adopt it.
**A:** The teams resisted: configuring the tool's parameters felt like extra work, they did not understand how the routing algorithm worked, and they did not know which restrictions they could add to improve its suggestions. I worked with them to translate real-world constraints into platform settings. Example: Peruvian markets house many small businesses in one place, all distributor clients, but the system treated them as separate stops. Adding a "market cluster" restriction let the algorithm assign trucks much more efficiently on delivery days for those clients.
**R:** By 2025 all trucks used the system and routing time fell by 60%.
**Use for:** "Handling resistance to change", "Working with a vendor", "Translating business requirements into technical ones", "A difficult stakeholder"

### 3. Digital financial ecosystem with BCP / Yape (partnership, rollout, fintech)
**S:** Delivery trucks at Alicorp's exclusive distributors collected cash, which created robbery risk, and settling each truck after its route at the distributor's admin office took a long time. Many shop owners also avoided holding cash because of street crime.
**T:** Design and roll out a digital payment and collection ecosystem for the traditional retail channel together with the bank.
**A:** I designed the ecosystem with the bank (BCP / Yape) and the sales area, and managed the rollout with the distributors alongside the bank's team: training truck crews and sales reps, field research with the UX/UI team, and improvements to the bank's app for a better distributor experience. A bank tool read each truck's deposits via WhatsApp and produced a pre-settlement report per truck, which made settlement much faster.
**R:** By the end of 2025, 80% of distributors had implemented it, covering 30% of total collections in Alicorp's exclusive-distributor channel. Faster settlement let trucks cover more delivery points per day, meaning more sales for the distributors.
**Use for:** Fintech roles, "A project with external partners", "Leading a rollout", "Field / user research"

### 4. Merchandising-audit app at adidas (digitizing a manual process)
**S:** Store audits at adidas Peru were stored in OneDrive, one folder per day and per store, which made auditing a store from the marketing team very cumbersome.
**T:** Build a better way to capture and review in-store merchandising execution.
**A:** I designed the audit survey with the category managers, the head of visual merchandising and the omnichannel director; moved it into a mobile app; developed the web dashboard version and its KPIs; and rolled it out to store teams (visual merchandising operators, store managers). Photos were captured through the survey during store visits and organized in a tagged repository that could be searched and filtered.
**R:** The survey covered 100% of in-store spaces across every store format in the country, by gender, sport category and garment type, replacing folder-by-folder searching with searchable, filterable audits. The data was then used to prove compliance with adidas's global brand guidelines, report campaign implementation to the region, audit store spaces remotely, and audit the service level of the outsourced visual merchandising team.
**Use for:** "Digitalizing a manual process", "Launching a tool to users", "Working with senior stakeholders", "Managing an outsourced team", retail / sports tech roles

### 5. Campaign performance dashboard in Power BI at adidas (standardizing data, BI)
**S:** At adidas Peru, each marketing specialist calculated campaign metrics with their own formulas and different data, so campaign results across the two sales channels (own retail and wholesale) were not comparable.
**T:** Lead a data analytics project to give the company one consistent view of campaign performance (2022-2023).
**A:** I standardized a template that every specialist filled in with their campaign's performance, and implemented a Power BI dashboard with a data consultant. The dashboard pulled the data from the templates and partially automated the data download and transformation.
**R:** The dashboard updated daily, so different teams across the company could track campaign results in one place with the same metrics. ### 6. RPA for supermarket invoice registration at Alicorp (process automation with a vendor)
**S:** Invoices from supermarket chains, Alicorp's large modern-trade clients, were registered through a manual process in the Sales Operations area (2019-2020).
**T:** As Sales Operations Analyst I led the automation of this registration.
**A:** I mapped the process end to end, designed and documented the automatable process, then received and implemented the RPA (robotic process automation) built by an external software vendor.
**R:** Invoice registration ran automatically instead of manually. [Add if you remember: hours saved per week, volume of invoices, error reduction.]
**Use for:** "Process improvement", "Show initiative", "Working with a software vendor", automation / RPA questions

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the team, and how do you divide work?"
- "What does the development/project lifecycle look like, from idea to production?"
- "How do you onboard new team members?"

### About Tech & Growth
- "What's your current tech stack for [relevant area]?"
- "Is there room to grow into more architectural or strategic decisions?"
- "How does the team stay current with new tools and methods?"

### About Culture (use these to prevent disappointment)
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "Is there flexibility for remote/hybrid work?"
- "What's the balance between development/new projects and maintenance work?"
- "How would you describe the leadership style in this team?"
- "What do people who thrive here have in common?"

## Phone/Video Interview Tips
- Have STAR examples written out (use this file)
- Keep a glass of water nearby
- Smile when speaking (it changes your tone)
- Ask for clarification if a question is vague
- It's OK to take 5 seconds to think before answering
- End with: "Is there anything else you'd like to know about my background?"

## After the Application (Best Practice)

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- **Don't call to "stand out"** or to learn more about the role post-submission - this risks a negative impression
- If the employer specified a timeline, respect it and wait
- If no timeline was given and significant time has passed (2+ weeks), a brief call to ask about status is acceptable
- If you have genuinely new, relevant information to share, a short follow-up is fine

### Thank-You Notes
- When you receive any update (interview invitation, rejection, or status update), send a brief thank-you message
- Express appreciation for their time and the process
- Keep it short (2-3 sentences)

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Start with easy warm-up questions ("Tell me about yourself")
3. Progress to role-specific technical questions
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions using the competencies from the job posting
5. End with a tough question or curveball
6. After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to sharpen
7. Suggest which STAR example would work best for each question
